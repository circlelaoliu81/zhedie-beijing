Folded Beijing is a pocket-sized life sim about scraping by in a megacity. You land in Beijing with one suitcase and a little starting cash, and you have 100 days to survive on whatever trade you can pick up.

Five ways to make money, each with its own temper. Running a night-market stall means first working out which spot moves which goods — the same table of stock sells completely differently in a night market and in a park. Food delivery needs an e-bike; rainy days pay more but wreck your body. Day labor means standing at the roadside waiting for a van to pick you up, paid the same day, hard work and low pay but no entry cost. Ride-hailing requires a deposit to rent the car; the late shift earns well and costs you sleep. Livestreaming grows only through consecutive sessions, and brand deals arrive after the followers do.

A day is 24 hours, split between working and sleeping however you choose. The longer you work, the faster health drains. Skip sleep entirely and you wake up with health in freefall, accidents piling up, and after several nights your character starts dozing off — income drops to 80%, then 60%, and eventually you collapse on the spot, losing the medical bill and the whole day. Knowing when to stop matters more than what you do.

Every day brings a news item, a random event and one blunt line of commentary. Loans accrue daily interest; so do savings.

No network, no ads, no in-app purchases. Turn it on and play, progress saves automatically.
